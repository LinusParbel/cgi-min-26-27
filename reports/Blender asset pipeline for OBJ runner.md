# Hand-model the landmarks, palette-texture everything else

The fastest pipeline that holds up under grading has four parts. Both students first spend about a week on short, game-focused low-poly tutorials. They then hand-model every graded asset at real-world scale and texture all of them through **one shared palette PNG**, whose swatches each mesh's UVs point at. Everything goes out through a fixed OBJ export preset (**−Z forward, Y up, Normals, UVs, Triangulated Mesh on, MTL off, object at the world origin**). Kenney's CC0 kits fill the background. Kenney is the single best external source because every kit checked ships OBJ files that all reference one 512×512 `colormap.png`. That is exactly the "one OBJ + one PNG" format the engine reads, so those files load with no Blender work at all. The hero assets should be your own: the e-scooter, the rider, Hannover-specific facades and the Kröpcke clock. That is where the "own 3D models" grade is earned, and no downloadable model of the Kröpcke-Uhr, the ÜSTRA trams or the Neues Rathaus exists anyway. AI assistance is practical in two narrow roles. Claude Code can write a reviewable headless export/validation script and generators for repetitive modular pieces. A Blender MCP server can act as a tutor that diagnoses scenes. Neither should be used as the modeler. Text-to-3D output is dense and messy, it isn't the students' work, and in one case (Hunyuan3D) its license excludes the EU. No course-specific AI policy was found, so the lecturer's answer to one email decides how far AI use may go. A register that sorts every asset into original, modified third-party or unmodified third-party, with title, author, source and license (TASL) credits and kept `.blend` files, is what makes authorship verifiable.

## A one-week tutorial sprint beats the four-hour donut

The well-known Blender Guru donut series was rebuilt for Blender 4.0. It runs about **4 h 29 min** and spends roughly half of that on sculpting, shading nodes, compositing and rendering ([Blender Guru](https://www.blenderguru.com/tutorials/blender-4-beginner-donut-tutorial)). None of that carries over to a Phong engine that reads OBJ files. Two shorter game-oriented sources fit better. The first is Polygon Runway's free "Full Blender 4 Course for Complete Beginners", which covers interface, modifiers and a first low-poly scene ([BlenderNation Bazaar](https://bazaar.blendernation.com/listing/full-blender-4-course-for-complete-beginners-2024-polygon-runway/)). The second is Grant Abbitt's free **Blender 4.2 low-poly well** series, about 13 minutes per part ([Class Central](https://www.classcentral.com/course/youtube-beginner-blender-4-2-tutorial-modelling-a-low-poly-well-part4-326168); [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=OlnkGCdtGEw)), which also has a written Blender 4.5 version ([Substack](https://grantabbitt.substack.com/p/create-a-low-poly-well-in-blender)). Two follow-ups add the techniques this project needs. Abbitt's "Essential Tips for Low Poly Scenes" covers triangles versus n-gons and keeping the object count down ([BlenderNation](https://www.blendernation.com/2024/11/05/essential-tips-for-low-poly-scenes-blender-tutorial/)). Imphenzia's "Step by Step Low Poly Modeling in Blender 4.4" teaches the palette-texture workflow, and its palette texture is public domain ([YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0GPJCdYCLnA)). The palette workflow suits this engine best of anything in the notes. Imphenzia's "10 low poly cars in 10 minutes" shows the box-modeling vocabulary that transfers directly to scooters and trams ([BlenderNation](https://www.blendernation.com/2020/07/07/speed-modeling-10-low-poly-cars-in-10-minutes/)). A reasonable schedule, which is our own estimate since no source gives beginner timings, is: interface on day 1, the well on day 2, palette texturing on day 3, two or three real props pushed all the way into the engine on days 4–5, and one larger Mirror/Array asset on days 6–7.

Older tutorials have one trap. **Blender 4.1 removed the mesh "Auto Smooth" checkbox** and replaced it with a "Smooth by Angle" modifier. In Object Mode the equivalent command is "Shade Smooth by Angle", and in Edit Mode it is "Set Sharpness by Angle" ([Blender 4.1 release notes](https://developer.blender.org/docs/release_notes/4.1/modeling/)). Pre-4.1 videos that tell you to tick Auto Smooth are pointing at a control that no longer exists ([CG Cookie](https://blog.cgcookie.com/?p=17205)). Since Blender 4.2, most add-ons that used to ship with Blender install with one click from the Extensions platform ([CG Cookie](https://cgcookie.com/posts/everything-new-in-blender-4-2)). That includes **LoopTools**, which is useful for circular wheel holes ([Blender manual issue](https://projects.blender.org/blender/blender-manual/issues/104878)), and **Bool Tool** for window cut-outs ([Renderjuice](https://www.renderjuice.com/blender/extensions/bool-tool)). Geometry Nodes and building-generator add-ons don't pay off in 4–6 weeks. Arrays over a hand-made kit give the same result, and generator output also weakens any claim that the model is your own work.

### Every graded asset starts as a primitive blockout on the 1 m grid

The safe modifier set for this engine is **Mirror, Array, Bevel (1–2 segments), Solidify and simple Booleans**. The OBJ exporter can apply modifiers at export time ([Blender manual: OBJ](https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/latest/files/import_export/obj.html)). Subdivision Surface should stay off or at level 1, because each level multiplies the triangle count by four. One engine fact shapes how moving parts are built. `ObjLoader` **merges every object in a file into one mesh and ignores groups, materials and smoothing groups** ([ObjLoader.cs](../Engine/Geometry/Loaders/ObjLoader.cs)). Anything the game code must move on its own therefore needs its own OBJ with its own pivot: scooter wheels, handlebar, rider limbs.

| Asset | Fastest beginner approach | Est. time (our estimate) |
|---|---|---|
| Cone, barrier, bench, lamp, bin | 1–3 primitives. Bench slats via Array, legs via Mirror. Bin from a tapered cube plus 8–12-sided cylinder wheels. Colour bands are faces moved onto palette swatches. | 0.5–2.5 h each |
| E-scooter | Beveled cube deck, tilted 8–12-sided cylinder stem, mirrored handlebar. Wheels as **separate OBJs with the origin on the axle** so code can spin them. | 4–8 h |
| Rider | Blocky segmented figure (helmet hides the face) modeled in riding pose, split with P into 6–10 rigid parts, each origin at its joint, animated by matrices in code. | 1–2 days |
| Facade/street kit | Fixed footprints, e.g. 4 m × 3.5 m storey modules plus a 20 m street segment. Origin at a consistent corner, tiling previewed with Array. | 2–3 days |
| Kröpcke clock | Photo references in orthographic views. Stacked 8–16-sided prisms, inset glazed panels, clock faces, domed cap from a half UV sphere or a Screw-modifier profile. | 5–10 h |

For the kit, three practitioner rules matter. The Level Design Book says "**do not make variants yet**": build the basic pieces at human scale inside a fixed bounding box, then get variety through texture swaps ([Level Design Book](https://book.leveldesignbook.com/process/blockout/metrics/modular)). Bethesda's Skyrim kit talk recommends large grid snaps ([GDC 2013 slides](https://www.slideshare.net/slideshow/gdc2013-kit-buildingfinal/17728576)). Polycount practitioners model to the grid rather than relying on vertex snapping, and add 25/50/75 cm filler pieces for widths that fall off the grid ([Polycount](https://polycount.com/discussion/comment/2688653)). For the rider, Imphenzia's beginner low-poly character tutorial ([YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=K84MA4e7S38)) and Abbitt's PS1-style character video ([Class Central](https://www.classcentral.com/course/youtube-playstation-1-characters-in-blender-486471)) are the right scale of ambition. The engine has no skeletal animation, so their rigging chapters can be skipped.

The Kröpcke clock rewards research before modeling. It began in **1885 as a multifunctional weather column ("Wettersäule") by Conrad Oertel**, designed to echo the dome of the neighbouring Café Robby. Its helmet-like cap sat over the clockwork, and it was built from steel frames, shaped sheet metal and glazed panels. It was demolished in 1954 and replaced in 1955 by a Karl Cravatzo design. The clock standing today is a **1977 replica of the 1885 original** and has served as an art display case since around 2005 ([de.wikipedia](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kr%C3%B6pcke-Uhr)). No source gave its height or number of faces, so estimate proportions from photos that include people. Stylize the shape: exaggerate the cap and clock faces so they read at runner speed. The tram is the same kind of job. The **TW 2000 is 25.82 m long and 2.65 m wide** ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TW_2000)), which makes it a straightforward box-modeling exercise from photos.

## One shared palette PNG removes the one-texture-per-mesh constraint

Imphenzia-style **palette texturing** is the right default. You scale a face's UVs "down to a tiny number and drag the entire thing into the color" on a small swatch image ([Babylon.js forum](https://forum.babylonjs.com/t/uv-mapping-seems-to-be-blending-colors-from-the-texture/31632)). Commercial low-poly packs work this way, typically using "one material with a texture atlas containing color palettes laid out in a grid" ([Unity Discussions](https://discussions.unity.com/t/released-palette-modifier-change-the-colors-of-low-poly-models-directly-inside-unity/760556)). Gradient strips inside each swatch fake lighting and shadow at almost no memory cost ([80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/elevating-gradient-texturing-to-a-standard-workflow-in-blender)). The method needs almost no unwrapping skill. It lets a whole multi-part object share one PNG, which suits a loader that merges objects anyway, and recolouring an asset means editing pixels, not geometry. The free extension **Dynamic Color Palette** automates the UV moves ([extensions.blender.org](https://extensions.blender.org/add-ons/dynamic-color-palette/)). A CC0 gradient atlas is also available ([itch.io](https://tenebra23.itch.io/texture-gradient-palette)). A **trim sheet** is the professional choice for large unique geometry. Polycount practitioners note that it moves the effort into careful UV layout ([Polycount](https://polycount.com/discussion/comment/2593030)), which is probably too much for two beginners unless one of them owns the buildings full time. **Baking to a single texture** is mainly for flattening downloaded multi-material models (see the asset-sources section).

This engine adds one constraint that the tutorials don't mention. `Texture.FromFile` hard-codes **Linear magnification and LinearMipmapLinear minification**, with Repeat wrapping and no option for Closest filtering ([Texture.cs](../Engine/OpenGL/Texture.cs)). Palette tutorials recommend Closest filtering for crisp edges ([BlenderNation Bazaar](https://bazaar.blendernation.com/listing/this-blender-scene-uses-only-one-texture-texture-atlas-colour-palette-tutorial/)). Here, a UV point near a swatch border blends neighbouring colours, and distant mip levels average adjacent swatches together. That matches the colour-bleeding complaints in the Babylon thread. Our practical rule, inferred from the filter settings: use a **256² or 512² PNG with large solid swatches of 16–32 px or more**, and keep collapsed UVs in the centre of each swatch. Stick to power-of-two sizes and PNG over JPG, because JPG compression smears swatch edges.

For UV basics, beginners need four operators. **Smart UV Project** is "suited to mechanical objects and architecture". **Mark Seam + Unwrap** handles curved props. **Project from View** suits flat facades and signs. **Lightmap Pack** on a new UV map is used only for baking ([Blender manual: UV](https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/latest/modeling/meshes/editing/uv.html)). Repeat wrapping lets a road or pavement mesh tile a CC0 texture with UVs outside 0–1. It also means that mesh can't share an atlas.

The engine has no shadows. The one safe way to fake them is to bake **ambient occlusion**, which "ignores scene lights", and multiply it into the base colour ([Blender manual: baking](https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/latest/render/cycles/baking.html)). Baked directional light would contradict the moving point light. AO conflicts with palettes, though, because collapsed palette UVs leave no unique texel space ([UV-Colorize README](https://github.com/RouBroeck/UV-Colorize)). Choose per asset: shared palette without AO, or a unique baked texture with AO for one or two hero props. On colour, the engine uploads `Rgba8` with **no sRGB conversion** ([Texture.cs](../Engine/OpenGL/Texture.cs)). This is the consistent "no sRGB anywhere" mode: correct as long as textures and framebuffer agree, though lighting is computed on gamma-encoded values ([Khronos forum](https://community.khronos.org/t/opengl-and-srgb-confusion/76300)). Blender's AgX/Filmic viewport therefore looks softer than the game. Preview with the View Transform set to **Standard** and tune colours in the PNG. Re-check this after future `lecture-XX` merges, in case sRGB support is added.

## Six export settings and four habits prevent most breakage

Blender's C++ exporter (File > Export > Wavefront .obj) defaults to `forward_axis='NEGATIVE_Z'`, `up_axis='Y'`, `apply_modifiers=True`, `apply_transform=True`, `export_uv=True` and `export_normals=True`. Two of its defaults are wrong for this engine: **`export_triangulated_mesh=False` and `export_materials=True`** ([Blender Python API](https://docs.blender.org/api/current/bpy.ops.wm.html)).

| Setting | Value | Why, for this engine |
|---|---|---|
| Forward / Up | −Z / Y | Matches the engine's Y-up convention (exporter default) |
| Scale | 1.0 | Model at 1 unit = 1 m. Never fix size with object scale. |
| Apply Modifiers | On | The engine can't evaluate Mirror, Array or Bevel |
| UV Coordinates / Normals | On / On | Without `vt`, UVs become (0,0). Without `vn`, everything is flat-shaded. |
| **Triangulated Mesh** | **On** | The loader fan-triangulates, which breaks on concave n-gons from Booleans and insets |
| Write MTL | Off | `.mtl` is ignored |
| Selected Only | On per part | One OBJ = one mesh = one texture |

The loader explains most of these choices. It fan-triangulates polygons, gives faces without `vn` a flat face normal, assigns UV (0,0) when `vt` is missing, and deduplicates corners by their (position, UV, normal) triple. Because of that deduplication, the split normals from Shade Flat, Smooth by Angle or Weighted Normal reach the GPU intact ([ObjLoader.cs](../Engine/Geometry/Loaders/ObjLoader.cs)). The exporter writes "face or vertex normals, depending on the smooth setting" ([Blender manual](https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/latest/files/import_export/obj.html)). Pure flat shading is the cheapest stylized look. Smooth by Angle at roughly 30–40° with small bevels catches Phong highlights better.

The pitfalls come from transforms and normals. **Apply Transform bakes world-space positions into the vertices** ([Blender manual](https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/latest/files/import_export/obj.html)). An object sitting at x = 5 in the .blend therefore exports 5 m off-centre, and a wheel exported in place spins around the scene origin. The standard habit covers this. Run Ctrl+A > All Transforms, because unapplied scale is the most-reported import problem ([Unity Discussions](https://discussions.unity.com/t/blender-to-unity-normals-just-fine-in-blender-but-screwed-up-in-unity/177468)). Set the origin deliberately: bottom-centre for props, a fixed corner for kit pieces, the axle for wheels, the joint for limbs ([StraySpark checklist](https://www.strayspark.studio/blog/game-ready-asset-checklist-blender-2026)). Move the object to the world origin. Never mirror with negative scale. The engine culls back faces with counter-clockwise front faces, so a flipped face doesn't look dark, it **disappears**. Check the Face Orientation overlay (blue means outward) and run Shift+N before every export. The first test should come in week one: a deliberately asymmetric prop, such as a cone with an arrow, loaded through `ObjLoader` under the single point light. It confirms axes, scale and shading before twenty assets depend on them. Store `.blend` sources next to the exported OBJ/PNG pairs in `RunnerGame/Assets/`, and never hand-edit an OBJ.

### Converting a multi-material model to one PNG

First check whether the model already uses one texture. If it does, the job is: import the GLB, join the parts with Ctrl+J, apply transforms, set the origin, export. Kenney itself prefers GLB or FBX over OBJ in general ([Kenney knowledge base](https://kenney.nl/knowledge-base/game-assets-3d/importing-3d-models-into-game-engines)), but its OBJs are exactly what this engine needs. For flat-colour models, as in old Kenney and many Quaternius packs, the fastest route needs no baking. Select the faces of each material slot, scale their UVs to roughly zero onto the matching palette swatch, then delete every material slot but one. Textured or procedural models need a bake:

1. Join the parts.
2. Add a second UV map and run Lightmap Pack with a margin.
3. Put the *same* target image in an Image Texture node in **every** material and make it the active node. "If a material lacks one, nothing is baked for it."
4. Bake **Diffuse with only Color enabled**, which gives the unlit base colour ([Blender manual: baking](https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/latest/render/cycles/baking.html)).
5. Make the new UV map the active one before export, because the exporter writes only the active layer.

Fully metallic materials may bake dark in the Color pass. Test one, and if it comes out black, set Metallic to 0 or route Base Color through Emission and bake Emit instead. The free GPL **Material Combiner** add-on builds a single atlas automatically. Install it from the master-branch zip, not from Releases ([GitHub](https://github.com/Grim-es/material-combiner-addon)). Command-line converters such as `assimp export in.glb out.obj` change the file format but don't merge textures ([TechOverflow](https://techoverflow.net/2025/03/31/how-to-convert-dae-file-to-obj-on-the-command-line/)).

## AI works as tutor and pipeline scripter, not as modeler

Live Blender MCP is mature enough to install. The community server, now **`mcp-for-blender` 2.1.9 (6 Oct 2026, MIT)**, supports Blender 3.0+ and registers in Claude Code with `claude mcp add blender uvx mcp-for-blender` ([PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/mcp-for-blender/); [GitHub](https://github.com/ahujasid/blender-mcp)). It runs arbitrary Python, takes multi-angle viewport screenshots, and pulls models from Poly Haven, Sketchfab and Poly Pizza or generates them with Tripo, Hunyuan3D and Rodin. Blender's own **Lab MCP server (v1.0.3, needs Blender 5.1+)** is narrower. It focuses on Python API and manual lookup and scene exploration, and Blender warns that it "will execute LLM generated code in Blender **without any guards**" ([blender.org](https://www.blender.org/lab/mcp-server/)). Anthropic added a Blender connector for Claude on 28 Apr 2026 ([Unite.ai](https://www.unite.ai/anthropic-wires-claude-into-photoshop-blender-and-ableton/)).

The security posture needs attention:

- The community add-on's socket has **no authentication**.
- Anonymous telemetry is **on by default**. Disable it with `DISABLE_TELEMETRY=true`. `BLENDER_MCP_SAFE_MODE=1` blocks file, network and process access ([GitHub](https://github.com/ahujasid/blender-mcp)).
- **CVE-2026-10688** (CVSS 5.5) covers unsanitised code injection through `execute_blender_code` ([NVD](https://nvd.nist.gov/vuln/detail/CVE-2026-10688)). Whether 2.1.x fixes it is unverified.

If you use it: set both environment variables, pin the version (ten releases in three weeks), approve every code call yourself, and keep the `.blend` files in git.

Evidence on quality is thin and mostly comes from vendors, but it agrees. LLM-written `bpy` geometry handles primitive-based, hard-surface and architectural shapes. It fails at organic forms, edge flow, precise placement and "visual judgment of proportions" ([MindStudio](https://www.mindstudio.ai/blog/claude-blender-mcp-real-world-performance)). One builder reports that blind execution "produces garbage after 3-4 steps". Feeding a viewport screenshot back after each step was "the single biggest quality improvement", and the verdict was "Is it production-ready? No." ([Blender devtalk](https://devtalk.blender.org/t/3d-agent-blender-ai-assistant-built-a-multi-agent-system-on-top-of-blenders-mcp-and-python-api-and-sharing-architecture-learnings-for-the-lab-discussion/44260)). In the same thread, Brecht recommended steering MCP toward explaining errors, suggesting export settings and running scene checklists. No independent benchmark of quality, time or token cost exists.

The better AI pattern for this project is **headless generator and export scripts** run with `blender --background --python script.py` ([skills.sh](https://skills.sh/terminalskills/skills/blender-scripting)). One validated script can apply transforms, check origins, normals and UVs, and call `wm.obj_export` with `export_triangulated_mesh=True, export_materials=False` for every asset. It is deterministic, reviewable in git, needs no listening socket and costs nothing in screenshots. Parametric generators also suit the truly repetitive pieces, such as street-segment variants and facade window arrays, because exact metre tiling is a measurable spec. Text/image-to-3D is the weakest option. Generated meshes run to **500K+ polygons for simple objects** ([levelup](https://levelup.gitconnected.com/building-game-ready-3d-assets-fast-the-best-ai-generators-for-production-workflows-in-2026-f1f725ff5df0)), and one Tripo model needed retopology from 1,482,050 triangles to 11,748 quads ([Scenario](https://help.scenario.com/articles/4692888910-tripo-family-the-complete-guide)). Meshy's free outputs are CC BY 4.0 ([Meshy](https://docs.meshy.ai/en/webapp/pricing)). Hunyuan3D's community license limits its territory to the world **excluding the EU, UK and South Korea** ([Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/tencent/Hunyuan3D-2/blob/main/LICENSE)), which rules it out for a team in Germany. For a cone or a bin, cleaning a generated mesh down to a few hundred triangles with a palette UV takes longer than modeling the object.

Whether any of this counts as "own models" depends on the course. No policy from Leibniz Universität Hannover or this course was found. German practice puts the lecturer in charge. Uni Bremen treats undisclosed AI content as misconduct ([Uni Bremen](https://www.uni-bremen.de/fb8/ies/ba-integrierte-europastudien-1/neue-ki-richtlinie)). Uni Münster's FB6 requires a declaration naming the tools and how they were used, with prompts and outputs attached ([Uni Münster](https://www.uni-muenster.de/FB6/studium/umgang_mit_ki/index.html)). The defensible split, our judgment, is to hand-model, UV and texture every graded asset in the Blender UI. Claude Code then handles tooling (the export/validator script), scripted generators for repetitive kit pieces, which the students design and review, and MCP-as-tutor diagnostics. Generated meshes stay out of the submission, except as throwaway placeholders that get replaced. Send the lecturer one email that asks two things: whether AI-assisted scripting is allowed, and whether procedurally scripted geometry counts as an own model.

## A three-status asset register makes authorship gradeable

Each license has different obligations:

- **CC0** carries none, though Creative Commons still recommends crediting.
- **CC BY 4.0** requires credit and a license link, and you "must indicate if you modified the material and retain an indication of previous modifications" ([CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/)).
- **CC BY-SA** requires the modified asset to carry the same license ([OpenGameArt FAQ](https://opengameart.org/content/faq)). Keep those files in their own folder so the obligation visibly applies to them alone and not to the code.
- **CC BY-NC** is arguable for a university project, but anyone can fork a public repo, so avoid it.
- **ND** licenses conflict with modifying assets at all.
- **Sketchfab Standard and Synty** can't be committed under any reading.

This also follows the repo's own rule (CONTRIBUTING.md, summarised in CLAUDE.md) to add only assets "we are allowed to share".

Use Creative Commons' **TASL** format: Title, Author (as the licensor wants to be credited), Source (the full original link), License (named and linked). For adaptations, credit the original in full and state that your version is adapted ([CC attribution wiki](https://wiki.creativecommons.org/wiki/Best_practices_for_attribution)). In practice that means the following (the layout is our proposal; no university standard was found):

- **`RunnerGame/Assets/ASSETS.csv`** lists every file with:
  - status: `original`, `modified` or `unmodified`
  - origin title, author, source URL, license and download date or pack version
  - a modifications text, who made or changed it, triangle count and texture
- **`CREDITS.md`** holds a TASL line per third-party asset, e.g. *"e scooter" by Artbor, CC BY 4.0. Changes: converted glTF→OBJ, decimated, baked to single PNG.* For CC0 assets, credit "Kenney (www.kenney.nl)" as the License.txt suggests.
- **Folders** separate `Assets/Original/`, `Assets/Modified/` and `Assets/ThirdParty/`, and each pack's `License.txt` stays next to its files.
- **Proof of authorship** for the "own models" grade: keep the `.blend` sources, progress and before/after screenshots, and any generator scripts with prompts summarised in their commits.
- **AI log:** an `AI_USAGE.md` or report section names the tool, model and version (e.g. Claude Code, mcp-for-blender 2.1.9) and what was human versus AI work, in the form German policies like Münster's expect.
- **City model:** if you use it, credit it as "3D-Stadtmodell, Landeshauptstadt Hannover, FB Planen und Stadtentwicklung, Bereich Geoinformation, CC BY 4.0".

## Conclusion

The engine's limits turn out to help the project. The ignored `.mtl`, merged objects and single-texture rule look restrictive, but they push the team toward the shared palette PNG that professional low-poly packs already use. That keeps UV work trivial, makes Kenney's CC0 kits work with no Blender step, and frees beginner hours for the few assets graders will actually look at: the scooter, the rider and the Kröpcke clock. The same logic applies to AI. Its value here isn't speed in modeling. It is a deterministic export and validation script, which removes a whole class of pipeline bugs, and an inspectable record of authorship.

Three facts remain open and should be settled in week one rather than assumed. The exporter's −Z/Y default has to be checked against the engine's track direction with an asymmetric test object. The lecturer has to rule on AI-scripted geometry. And Weighted Normal custom normals have to be confirmed to survive "Apply Modifiers" in Blender 5.x, for which only forum evidence exists. Linear-only texture filtering is the one engine constraint without a workaround in Blender. If palette bleeding turns out to be visible at runner distances, a small, isolated, upstream-friendly engine change adding an optional nearest-filter flag to `Texture` is worth more than any amount of swatch padding.
